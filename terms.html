Please read this before relying on anything Fennel shows you.

What Fennel is

Fennel is a diary. You log meals, symptoms and stool form, and it reports how often your symptoms followed particular foods and FODMAP groups, inside fixed time windows, with the sample sizes shown. It is a counting and statistics tool built on your own records and on published, openly available food information.

What Fennel is not

  • It is not medical, dietary or nutritional advice.
  • It is not a diagnosis, and it does not treat anything.
  • It is not a verdict on whether any food is fit for you to eat.
  • It does not know your history, your medications or your other conditions.
  • It is not a substitute for a doctor, a gastroenterologist or a registered dietitian.

Association is not causation, and Fennel says so on every statistic

Everything the app reports is an association between two things you logged. A symptom that followed a meal may have been caused by that meal, by something else you ate, by stress, by sleep, by your cycle, or by nothing identifiable at all. Fennel shows the counts on both sides, names the window it used, and flags runs of days that were unusually stressful or fell in a cycle phase, so a reading can be checked rather than believed. It reports nothing at all until there is enough data behind it, and it says how much more it needs.

Fennel does not rate foods

This is the app's central rule rather than a disclaimer. It never labels a food, never colours one good or bad, and never tells you to avoid anything. Colour marks how strong the evidence is and nothing else. What to do about a pattern is a decision for you, with your clinician or dietitian.

The reintroduction program

The program schedules challenge days and rest days and records what you logged on them. It is bookkeeping to run alongside professional advice, not a protocol prescribed by this app. Phase lengths appear as ranges because the sources state them as ranges, and the sources are one tap away on the screen. Long term dietary restriction has real nutritional and microbiome costs, and the app says so where the elimination countdown lives. Please do not run a restrictive diet from an app alone.

Symptoms that need a person, not an app

If you log blood in your stool, a fever alongside gut symptoms, or unintended weight loss, Fennel shows one card suggesting you discuss it with a clinician and cites where that comes from. It will not tell you what it might mean, because it cannot know. If you feel unwell or have any symptom that worries you, contact your clinician or your local emergency service. Do not use this app to decide whether to seek care.

Accuracy of the food information

Food tags are drawn from the public sources listed on the Sources page and each one links the source on the screen that makes the claim. FODMAP content varies with variety, ripeness, portion and preparation, and published figures differ between sources. Some rows in the food list are marked as under review while a registered dietitian works through them; those rows carry the same citations as every other row, and any tag can be reported as wrong. All of it is a snapshot taken when the app version was built.

Your data is yours

Everything you log stays on your device and you can export all of it as a CSV file at any time, free. See the Privacy Policy. There is no sync in this version, so a lost or wiped phone means a lost diary: export it if it matters to you.
